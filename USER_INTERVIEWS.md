# User Interviews

Three interviews conducted during the week of May 20–24, 2026 via in-person and WhatsApp conversations. Each interview was 10–15 minutes.

---

## Interview 1 — A.K., Developer, Early-Stage Startup

**Role:** Full-stack developer at a 6-person startup
**Company stage:** Seed stage, ~6 months old
**Date:** May 21, 2026

**Notes:**
A.K. is currently paying for both Cursor Pro and ChatGPT Plus separately. When asked why, he said he finds each tool better at different things — Cursor for in-editor coding and ChatGPT for broader reasoning and writing tasks.

**Direct quotes:**
- "I pay for both Cursor and ChatGPT Plus because each is good at different things."
- "I never really thought about whether one could replace the other — I just kept both."
- "If a tool could tell me I'm doubling up, I'd actually listen to it."

**Most surprising thing:**
He had never considered that Cursor's chat feature largely overlaps with ChatGPT Plus for coding tasks. When shown the audit output flagging this overlap with exact dollar savings, he immediately asked when the tool would be live and said he'd share it with his team.

**What it changed about the design:**
Reinforced the decision to show exact dollar amounts on the overlap detection cards rather than just a generic warning. Users respond to specific numbers, not abstract advice.

## Interview 2 — R.M., Frontend Developer, Freelance Intern

**Role:** Frontend developer and designer, interning at a design agency
**Company stage:** Individual freelancer / intern
**Date:** May 22, 2026

**Notes:**
R.M. uses a mix of AI tools for frontend work and design ideation but admitted he had never added up what he was spending monthly across all of them.

**Direct quotes:**
- "I honestly don't track my AI spending monthly."
- "I just pay whatever the subscription is and forget about it."
- "Seeing the total number laid out like that was kind of shocking actually."

**Most surprising thing:**
When shown a mock audit of his stack, he was visibly surprised by the total monthly number. He had been thinking of each tool as "just $20" without adding them together. The annual savings number landed harder than the monthly number.

**What it changed about the design:**
Confirmed that showing annual savings prominently in the hero section was the right call. Monthly spend feels abstract — annual spend feels real. Also added a "you currently spend $X/month total" line to make the baseline visible before showing savings.

## Interview 3 — S.P., Developer, Freelance

**Role:** Full-stack developer, freelancing
**Company stage:** Solo freelancer
**Date:** May 22, 2026

**Notes:**
S.P. had already made the switch from GitHub Copilot to Cursor on his own, but was still unclear about whether he had fully cancelled Copilot. He mentioned Copilot felt redundant once he started using Cursor daily.

**Direct quotes:**
- "Copilot became useless for me after I started using Cursor."
- "I think I cancelled it but I am not 100% sure — I should check."
- "A tool that automatically flags this kind of overlap is crazy useful, it could save a lot of money."

**Most surprising thing:**
He was not sure if he had actually cancelled Copilot despite having stopped using it months ago. This suggests a real pattern — people switch tools but forget to cancel old subscriptions. The audit tool catching this zombie subscription pattern is genuinely valuable.

**What it changed about the design:**
Added stronger language on the overlap detection cards. Also reinforced the value of the audit for catching forgotten subscriptions, not just active overlap.
