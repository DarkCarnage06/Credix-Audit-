# Go-To-Market Strategy

## Exact Target User

Not "startups" — the specific user is an **engineering manager or founding engineer at a 5-25 person Series A startup** who:
- Has 3-8 developers on the team
- Is paying for at least 3 AI tools across the team
- Gets the monthly bill, sighs, and pays it without analyzing it
- Has never benchmarked their AI spend against alternatives
- Job title: Engineering Manager, VP Engineering, CTO, or Lead Developer

Secondary user: **freelance developer or indie hacker** paying for 2-3 AI tools personally and not tracking the overlap.

## How to Get the First 100 Users in 30 Days With $0

**Week 1 — Hacker News Show HN post**
Write a Show HN: "I built a free tool that audits your AI tool spend and finds overlap". HN loves free tools that solve real problems. Target: 50-100 upvotes, 20-30 comments, 200-500 visitors.

**Week 2 — Post in r/cursor and r/ChatGPT**
"I kept paying for Cursor and Copilot at the same time without realizing the overlap — so I built a tool to catch this automatically." Genuine story, links to the tool. Target: 10-20 upvotes per post, 50-100 visitors each.

**Week 3 — Cold DM on X**
Find people tweeting about AI tool costs or asking "is X worth it". Reply with the tool. 50 targeted DMs, expect 5-10 clicks each. Target: 50-100 visitors.

**Week 4 — Indie Hackers post**
Write a build-in-public post about building the tool in 7 days. Show the audit engine logic, the pricing research. Indie Hackers loves transparency. Target: 50-100 upvotes, 100-200 visitors.

## The Unfair Distribution Channel

Credex already has relationships with companies that oversold AI infrastructure credits. Those companies know exactly which startups are overspending on AI tools. A direct outreach from Credex to those startups — "we noticed you might be overpaying, here's a free audit" — is a warm intro that no competitor can replicate. The tool becomes a Trojan horse: free value first, Credex consultation second.
